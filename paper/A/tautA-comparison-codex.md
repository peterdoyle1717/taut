# Independent comparison of three Taut fillings version-A drafts

I compared only:

- `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex`
- `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex`
- `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex`

I did not read `tautA-comparison-claude.md` or `tautA2.tex`.

## Ranking

1. **`tautA-codex-01.tex`**: best spine for version 02. It keeps the full theorem statement, fixes several prose and typo issues, gives the most rigorous eligible-tet counting argument, and retains an explicit clean-complex verification.
2. **`tautA-claude-01.tex`**: mathematically close and sometimes fuller in flavor, but less polished and with stale comments plus a real typo in the triangulation discussion.
3. **`tautA-gpt-01.tex`**: cleanest and shortest prose, but mathematically weaker in the crucial Theorem 2 proof because it compresses cleanness into a gluing sketch instead of preserving the explicit pseudomanifold/link verification.

## Notation and setup

All three consistently use `M` for fillings in the basic definition: Claude `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:197`, Codex `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:198`, GPT `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:190`.

The support notation `K(M)` is best in Codex: it defines `K(M)` as the pure complex generated by the unoriented support of `M`, then defines simplicial and clean chains immediately after `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:782`. Claude has the same convention but with more words and a slightly awkward parenthetical `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:797`. GPT is compact and clear about chains versus complexes `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:718`, but its Triangulations section switches the ambient complex variable to `K`, which may visually compete with `K(M)` `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:683`.

The Triangulations section is live in all three. Claude and Codex still contain stale commented-out explanatory material in the abstract/overview: Claude `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:183` and `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:262`; Codex `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:184` and `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:263`. GPT removes these comments entirely.

The clean-complex convention is correctly present in all three: purity, pseudomanifold boundary triangle condition, and connected links in codimension at least two. Claude states it at `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:728`; Codex at `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:729`; GPT at `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:694`. Codex is the best phrased version for v02 because it is precise without Claude's conversational digressions. GPT's explicit dimension-3 paraphrase is useful: connected edge links and connected vertex links `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:699`.

Carrier typo: Claude has "would requires" at `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:786`; Codex fixes it to "would require" at `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:771`. GPT avoids the sentence and states the ordinary simplicial convention cleanly at `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:714`.

## Theorem 2

All three theorem statements preserve the essential conclusion that `M` is clean and `K(M)` triangulates `B^3`: Claude `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:815`, Codex `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:792`, GPT `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:727`. Codex's statement is the most compact and explicit because it says `M` is a taut filling of `X(\sigma)` `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:793`.

The proof should use Codex as spine. It introduces "filling pair" and "good" cleanly `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:799`, reduces by minimality and connected-sum splitting `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:805`, handles both post-flip cases, rules out multiplicity-2, and then explicitly checks the clean-complex conditions `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:877`.

The crucial cleanness issue:

- Claude keeps the explicit verification: after proving `M` is simplicial `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:876`, it checks pseudomanifold, disconnected edge links, and disconnected vertex links separately `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:883`.
- Codex also keeps the explicit verification and is the best version of it: pseudomanifold at `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:877`, edge links at `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:890`, vertex links at `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:904`, and the final contradiction at `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:915`.
- GPT does **not** keep the explicit verification. It collapses cleanness into a gluing paragraph beginning "It remains only to spell out why this gluing preserves cleanness" `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:806`. This is the main mathematical weakness of GPT: the pseudomanifold, edge-link, and vertex-link obstructions are sketched as possible persistent failures `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:812`, but not separately proved.

## Eligible-tet count

Claude gives a simple assignment of boundary faces to tets, with correct signs and support/copy warning `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:845`; it concludes at least `\maxdeg(\sigma)` disjointly eligible tets `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:851`. This is readable but slightly handwavy because the disjointness comes from assignment choices rather than a named counting variable.

Codex is most rigorous. It defines `A(u)` for each support tetrahedron, records only newly seen faces, and proves `f <= |supp M|+r <= |M|+r`, hence `r >= \maxdeg(\sigma)` `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:821`. It also explicitly says these are support tetrahedra and correctly oriented boundary faces `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:813`.

GPT uses essentially the same reveal argument as Codex `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:753`, but is a little less careful: it says the sets cover boundary faces because each boundary face has nonzero coefficient with the right sign `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:754`; Codex's wording "prescribed sign" is cleaner and more tied to `X(\sigma)` `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:824`.

## Post-flip cases and multiplicity obstruction

All three distinguish the one-sphere case from the conjoined-spheres case. Claude: `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:856`; Codex: `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:839`; GPT: `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:767`.

All three preserve the important remark that `M-t` need not be clean as one complex in the conjoined case, because the common-edge link can be disconnected. Claude states this explicitly at `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:869`, Codex at `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:865`, GPT at `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:793`.

All three include the disjoint-eligible multiplicity-2 obstruction. Claude's version is explicit after the two cases `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:876`; Codex handles it inside each case with a second eligible tet `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:850` and `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:868`; GPT handles it similarly `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:782` and `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:801`. For v02, keep Codex's placement but add a single summarizing sentence afterward, as Claude does, that `M` is now known simplicial.

## Shelling

All three use the shucking idea. Claude names it explicitly and gives the clearest removal-order description `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:966`. Codex is concise and still sufficient `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:942`. GPT is shortest and more sketchlike `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:840`.

Monotone handling is best in Claude: it explains one-, two-, and three-face attachments and notes that the three-face case would bury an interior vertex, so vertex count never decreases `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:947`. Codex keeps the same point more briefly `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:930`. GPT says the three-face case is excluded by absence of internal vertices `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:830`, but that is too compressed. Use Codex as base, graft Claude's monotone explanation in polished form.

## Flag section

Codex has the cleanest flag/clique terminology: "flag complex (or clique complex)" `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:975`. Claude says "also called a clique complex" `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:993`. GPT omits the clique synonym `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:864`; restore it.

All three have the right minimal forbidden configurations: missing triangle, missing `K_4` tetrahedron, and `K_5` `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:998`. Codex phrases the `K_5` obstruction best: a `K_5` forces five `K_4` tetrahedra, the boundary of a 4-simplex, a closed `3`-sphere/cycle that cannot occur in a triangulated 3-ball `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:1008`. Claude says the five tets "would form an `S^3`" `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:1030`; GPT says "forming the boundary of a 4-simplex" `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:885`. Use Codex/GPT wording and keep the explicit `S^3`-not-in-`B^3` step.

The octahedral exceptional case is present but weak in all three. Claude says to treat the octahedron separately `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:1070`; Codex says the octahedral boundary's taut fillings are checked directly from the same flip alternatives `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:1048`; GPT similarly says it is the small exceptional case `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:913`. Version 02 needs either a real two-sentence direct check or a lemma/reference, not just "checked directly."

The `K_4` proof is most complete in Claude and Codex. Codex gives a concise obstruction analysis around five candidate flips and boundary faces `text/tautxy/tautA-codex-01.tex:1040`. Claude's version is a little more explicit about the interior edge and the two boundary faces `ABC` and `BCD` `text/tautxy/tautA-claude-01.tex:1073`. GPT has the same idea but is shortest `text/tautxy/tautA-gpt-01.tex:910`.

Codex is the best polished middle ground.
