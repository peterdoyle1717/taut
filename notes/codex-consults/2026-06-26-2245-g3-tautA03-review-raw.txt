   167	\begin{abstract}
   168	Let $\sigma$ be a simplicial triangulation of the 2-sphere,
   169	$X$ the associated integral 2-cycle.
   170	A filling of $X$ is an integral 3-chain $M$
   171	with $\del M = X$;
   172	a taut filling is one with minimal $L_1$-norm.
   173	We show that any taut filling 
   174	arises from an extension of $\sigma$ to
   175	a simplicial complex homeomorphic to the 3-ball.
   176	This complex is shellable, and `flag':
   177	every clique in its 1-skeleton occurs as a simplex.
   178	The key to the proof is the general
   179	fact that any taut filling of an $n$-cycle
   180	splits under disjoint union, connected sum, and more generally
   181	what we call almost disjoint union,
   182	where summands are supported on sets that overlap in at most $n+1$ vertices.
   183	\end{abstract}

   203	For any taut filling $M$ of $X$ we have $\vertices(M) = \vertices(X)$.
   204	(Cf.\ Proposition \ref{nointernal} below.)
   205	This is why we write $C_n$ and $Z_n$ without reference to $\Omega$.
   206	
   207	Call an $n$-cycle $X+Y \in Z_n$ an \emph{almost disjoint union} if
   208	\[
   209	|\vertices(X) \cap \vertices(Y)| \leq n+1
   210	.
   211	\]
   212	This notion generalizes both disjoint union and connected sum
   213	along an $n$-simplex.
   214	Ellison \cite{ellison:gap}
   215	showed the $\Zvol$ adds under almost disjoint union:
   216	$\Zvol(X+Y) = \Zvol(X) + \Zvol(Y)$.
   217	This means that \emph{some} taut filling $M$ of $X$ splits into a sum
   218	$M=M_X+M_Y$ of taut fillings of $X,Y$.
   219	Here we amplify Ellison's Corollary 2 to show (Theorem \ref{th1})
   220	that when $n \geq 2$, \emph{any} taut filling of $X+Y$ splits.

   227	so in fact $\Qvol = \Rvol$.

   261	\section{Background}
   262	
   263	Our interest in $\Zvol$ stems from the work
   264	of Sleator, Tarjan, and Thurston
   265	\cite{stt:jams}.
   266	They observed that coning from a vertex of maximal degree
   267	shows that a $v$-vertex
   268	triangulation $\sigma$
   269	of $S^2$ has $\tetvol \leq 2v-4-\maxdeg(\sigma)$.
   270	If $v\geq13$ $\maxdeg(\sigma) \geq 6$, so $\tetvol \leq 2v-10$.
   271	They
   272	produced examples for which $\tetvol = 2v-10$, provided
   273	$v$ is larger than some unspecified bound,
   274	and conjectured that such examples should exist for any $v \geq 13$.
   275	Their approach was to 
   276	realize $\sigma$ as an ideal hyperbolic polyhedron
   277	with volume $2v V_0-O(\log(v))$,
   278	where $V_0$ is the volume of an
   279	equilateral ideal tetrahedron,
   280	which is maximal.
   281	This implies $\tetvol \geq 2v-O(\log(v))$.
   282	From there they bootstrapped their way up to showing
   283	that $\tetvol=2v-10$.
   284	They suggested
   285	\cite[p. 697]{stt:jams}
   286	that in fact $\Qvol= 2v-10$,
   287	which would immediately imply $\tetvol=2v-10$.
   288	Mathieu and Thurston \cite{mt}
   289	produced a class of examples,
   290	different from the examples of STT,
   291	for which they could show that $\Qvol = 2v-10$,
   292	still under the assumption that $v$
   293	is sufficiently large.
   294	In \cite{dew:filling} we produced examples for any $v \geq 13$ with
   295	$\Qvol=2v-10$, confirming the conjecture of Sleator, Tarjan, and Thurston.
   296	Theorem \ref{th2} here
   297	tells us that whenever we have $\Qvol=2v-10$,
   298	or just $\Zvol=2v-10$, any taut filling
   299	must arise from a triangulation of the ball.

   302	In \cite{dew:filling}
   303	we described triangulations of $S^2$ with $\Qvol < \Zvol$.

   307	$\lceil \Qvol \rceil = \Zvol$.

   317	\section{Taut fillings}
   318	
   319	We can think of an $n$-chain $X \in C_n$ as a multiset of
   320	non-cancelling oriented simplices:
   321	\[
   322	X = \sum_{t \in X} t
   323	.
   324	\]
   325	In this sum $t$ denotes an oriented simplex
   326	\[
   327	t=[x_0,\ldots,x_n]
   328	= [x_{\pi(0)},\ldots,x_{\pi(n)}]
   329	,\;\;\mbox{$\pi$ an even permutation}
   330	,
   331	\]
   332	and each unoriented simplex contributes a number of terms corresponding
   333	to its multiplicity.
   334	
   335	The size of $M$ as a multiset is its $L_1$-norm $|M|$.
   336	Write $U \subset M$
   337	if $U$ is a sub-multiset of $M$.
   338	This happens just when
   339	$|M| = |U| + |M-U|$.
   340	
   341	\begin{prop} \label{subtaut}
   342	If $M$ is taut and $U \subset M$ then $U$ is taut.
   343	\end{prop}
   344	
   345	\proofstart
   346	If $U$ is not taut, there is a smaller filling $V$ of $\del U$.
   347	Take $M^\prime = M-U+V$.
   348	In terms of multisets, this means that we substitute $V$ for $U$,
   349	and then do any required cancellation of oppositely oriented simplices
   350	of $M-U$ and $V$.
   351	We have
   352	\[
   353	\del M^\prime = \del M - \del U + \del V = \del M
   354	\]
   355	and
   356	\[
   357	|M^\prime| \leq |M-U| + |V| = |M| - |U| + |V| < |M|
   358	,
   359	\]
   360	contradiction.
   361	\proofend

   363	\section{Coning}
   364	For $x \in \Omega$, $U \in C_n$ let
   365	\[
   366	\nbhd(x,U) = \sum_{ t \in U: x \in \vertices(t) } t
   367	,
   368	\]
   369	\[
   370	\deg(x,U) = |\nbhd(x,U)|
   371	,
   372	\]
   373	\[
   374	\maxdeg(U) = \max_x \deg(x,U)
   375	.
   376	\]
   377	The \emph{cone from $x$ to $U$}
   378	is the $(n+1)$-chain
   379	\[
   380	\cone(x,U) = 
   381	\sum_{t \in U} \adj(x,t)
   382	,
   383	\]
   384	consisting of all the non-trivial oriented
   385	$(n+1)$-simplices $\adj(x,t)=[x x_0\ldots x_n]$ obtained
   386	by adjoining $x$ to  $t = [x_0\ldots x_n] \in U$.
   387	If $x \in \vertices(t)$ then $t$ does not contribute to the sum,
   388	so $|\cone(x,U)| = |U| - \deg(x,U)$.
   389	
   390	If $U$ is closed then
   391	$\del\, \cone(x,U) = U$, so
   392	
   393	\begin{prop} \label{maxdeg}
   394	For any $X \in Z_n$
   395	\[
   396	\Zvol(X) \leq |X| - \maxdeg(X)
   397	\mathproofend
   398	\]
   399	\end{prop}
   400	
   401	If $U \in Z_n$ and $x \notin \vertices(U)$ then
   402	$\deg(x,U)=0$ and
   403	$|\cone(x,U)| = |U|$.
   404	In this case we call $\cone(x,U)$ a \emph{complete cone}.
   405	By Proposition \ref{maxdeg} a non-trivial complete cone is not taut.
   406	(Equivalently, instead of coning from
   407	$x \notin U$, one could have coned from some $x \in U$.)
   408	Thus:
   409	\begin{prop}\label{nocone}
   410	If $M$ is taut it contains no non-trivial complete cone.
   411	\proofend
   412	\end{prop}
   413	
   414	Call $x \in \vertices(M) \setminus \vertices(\del M)$ an
   415	\emph{internal vertex} of $M$.
   416	If $x$ is internal to $M$ then $\nbhd(x,M)$ is a complete cone, so:
   417	\begin{prop} \label{nointernal}
   418	If $M$ is taut then
   419	it has no internal vertices.
   420	\proofend
   421	\end{prop}

   423	\section{Almost disjoint unions}
   424	
   425	Recall that if $X,Y \in Z_n$ we call $X+Y$ an almost disjoint union
   426	if
   427	\[
   428	|\vertices(X) \cap \vertices(Y)| \leq n+1
   429	.\]
   430	The most interesting special case is a connected sum,
   431	where $t=[c_0,c_1,\ldots,c_n]$ occurs once in $X$ and
   432	$-t=[c_1,c_0,\ldots,c_n]$ occurs once in $Y$.
   433	For example, if $n=1$ with $X$ a cycle of length $p$
   434	and $Y$ a cycle of length $q$
   435	the connected sum $X+Y$ is
   436	a cycle of length $p+q-2$.
   437	Here
   438	$\Zvol$ adds, because
   439	$\Zvol(X)=p-2$,
   440	$\Zvol(Y) = q-2$,
   441	and
   442	\[
   443	\Zvol(X+Y) = (p+q-2)-2 = \Zvol(X)+\Zvol(Y)
   444	.
   445	\]
   446	But not every filling of $X+Y$ splits, because a taut filling
   447	may contain any 2-simplex with vertices in $\vertices(X+Y)$.
   448	
   449	We want to show that fillings do split when $n \geq 2$.
   450	
   451	For $A \subset \Omega$, $p \in A$ define
   452	\[
   453	\pi_{A,p}: \Omega \to A
   454	\]
   455	\[
   456	\pi_{A,p}(x) = x \IF x \in A \ELSE p
   457	\]
   458	and let
   459	\[
   460	K_*(A,p): C_*(\Omega) \to C_*(A)
   461	\]
   462	be the induced chain map.
   463	This is a projection of $C_*(\Omega)$ onto $C_*(A)$.
   464	
   465	Let $A,B$ be finite vertex sets sharing the vertices
   466	$C = A \cap B$,
   467	and let $c=|C|$.
   468	Observe that
   469	$C_c(C)$ and $Z_{c-1}(C)$ are trivial,
   470	because $\Delta(C)$ contains no $c$-simplex,
   471	and (up to orientation) only one $c\mns 1$-simplex,
   472	with nothing to cancel its boundary.
   473	
   474	Let
   475	\[
   476	C_*(A,B) = C_*(A) \direct C_*(B)
   477	.
   478	\]
   479	For $p,q \in C$ define the mapping
   480	\[
   481	g_*(p,q) = K_*(A,p) \direct K_*(B,q):
   482	C_*(A \cup B) \to C_*(A,B)
   483	.
   484	\]
   485	
   486	\begin{prop} \label{recover}
   487	Let $(X,Y) \in C_n(A,B)$.
   488	If $|A \cap B| \leq n$
   489	we can recover $(X,Y)$ from $X+Y$.
   490	If $(X,Y) \in Z_n(A,B)$ this holds also when $|A \cap B|  = n+1$.
   491	\end{prop}
   492	
   493	\proofstart
   494	Let $C=A \cap B$.
   495	We can assume $|C| \geq 1$.
   496	(Add a brand new point to $C$ if necessary.)
   497	We claim that
   498	for any $p,q \in C$
   499	(not necessarily distinct) we have
   500	\[
   501	g_n(p,q)(X+Y)=(X,Y)
   502	.
   503	\]
   504	Indeed,
   505	\[
   506	K_n(A,p)(X+Y) = X + K_n(A,p)(Y)
   507	.
   508	\]
   509	The second term belongs to $C_n(C)$,
   510	which is trivial when $|C| \leq n$.
   511	If $Y \in Z_n(B)$ the second term belongs to $Z_n(C)$,
   512	which is trivial when $|C| \leq n+1$.
   513	\proofend
   514	
   515	
   516	\begin{theorem} \label{th1}
   517	If $|A \cap B| \leq n+1$,
   518	for all $(X,Y) \in Z_n(A,B)$ we have
   519	\[
   520	\Zvol(X+Y) = \Zvol(X)+\Zvol(Y)
   521	.
   522	\]
   523	And as long as $n \geq 2$,
   524	for any $M \in \taut(X+Y)$ 
   525	we have $M = M_X + M_Y$
   526	with $M_X \in \taut(X)$, $M_Y \in \taut(Y)$.
   527	\end{theorem}

   679	\section{Triangulations}
   680	
   681	We turn now to filling simplicial triangulations of $S^2$.
   682	We begin by fixing the terminology.
   683	
   684	An \emph{$n$-simplex} $s$ is a set of size $n+1$.
   685	Its \emph{faces} are its subsets,
   686	which are $k$-simplices with $-1 \leq k \leq n$,
   687	where the empty simplex has dimension $-1$.
   688	A \emph{simplicial complex} $\sigma$ is a finite collection of simplices
   689	closed under taking faces:
   690	if $t \subset s \in \sigma$ then $t \in \sigma$.
   691	
   692	For any simplex $s \in \sigma$ define the \emph{link}
   693	\[
   694	\link(s,\sigma) = \{t: t \cap s = \emptyset, s \cup t \in \sigma \}
   695	.
   696	\]
   697	This is a simplicial complex, but
   698	(except for $\link(\emptyset,\sigma)=\sigma$)
   699	it is not a subcomplex of $\sigma$,
   700	because we are taking simplices in $\sigma$ and lowering
   701	their dimension by $\dim s+1$.
   702	
   703	We are interested in normal pseudomanifolds,
   704	which we will call \emph{clean $n$-complexes}.
   705	A simplicial complex $\sigma$ is clean of dimension $n$ if:
   706	\begin{enumerate}
   707	\item
   708	$\sigma$ is pure of dimension $n$,
   709	meaning that every simplex of $\sigma$ is contained in some
   710	$n$-simplex of $\sigma$.
   711	
   712	\item
   713	$\sigma$ is a pseudomanifold:
   714	the link $\link(s,\sigma)$ of any $(n-1)$-simplex $s$
   715	consists of either one point
   716	(so $s$ is a boundary simplex)
   717	or two points
   718	(so $s$ is an interior simplex).
   719	
   720	\item
   721	$\sigma$ is normal:
   722	for every simplex $s$ of dimension $0,\ldots,n-2$,
   723	the link $\link(s,\sigma)$ is connected.
   724	\end{enumerate}
   725	The normality condition says that the $n$-simplices are glued along
   726	shared faces without additional identifications.  It rules out,
   727	for example, an icosahedron with a pair of opposite vertices identified.
   728	
   729	If $\sigma$ is clean, then each link $\link(s,\sigma)$ is clean.
   730	The boundary $\del \sigma$ of a clean complex is clean and closed:
   731	$\del \del \sigma = \{\emptyset\}$.
   732	
   733	If $W$ is an $n$-manifold, say that a simplicial complex $\sigma$
   734	is a \emph{simplicial triangulation} of $W$
   735	if the geometric carrier of $\sigma$ is homeomorphic to $W$.
   736	In this case $\sigma$ is necessarily clean.
   737	When $W$ is oriented, a choice of orientation determines one of the
   738	two associated cycles $X(\sigma) \in C_n$; our arguments do not depend
   739	on which of the two orientations is chosen.
   740	
   741	This notion of triangulation is not as general as one might want
   742	for some purposes.
   743	The double cover of a triangle is not a simplicial triangulation
   744	of $S^2$,
   745	because that would require two distinct
   746	$2$-simplices to have the same three edges.
   747	Nor can any $2$-simplex have two of its edges
   748	glued to one another.
   749	Thurston
   750	\cite{thurston:shapes}
   751	allows such triangulations,
   752	but it is unclear how important these are to
   753	his theory of shapes of surfaces.
   754	We do not allow them here.
   755	
   756	For a chain $M \in C_n$, define $K(M)$ to be the pure simplicial
   757	complex generated by the unoriented $n$-simplices on which $M$ has
   758	nonzero coefficient.
   759	We call $M$ \emph{simplicial} if every nonzero coefficient is $\pm 1$,
   760	so no unoriented $n$-simplex is repeated.
   761	We call $M$ \emph{clean} if $M$ is simplicial and $K(M)$ is a clean
   762	simplicial complex.
   763	
   764	\section{Filling a triangulation of the 2-sphere}
   765	
   766	\begin{theorem} \label{th2}
   767	If $\sigma$ is a simplicial triangulation of $S^2$ and $M$ is a taut
   768	filling of $X(\sigma)$, then $M$ is clean and $K(M)$ is a simplicial
   769	triangulation of $B^3$.
   770	\end{theorem}
   771	
   772	\proofstart
   773	Call $(\sigma,M)$ a \emph{filling pair} if $\sigma$ is a simplicial
   774	triangulation of $S^2$ and $M$ is a taut filling of $X(\sigma)$.
   775	Call a filling pair \emph{good} if $M$ is clean and $K(M)$ is a
   776	simplicial triangulation of $B^3$.
   777	We will show that every filling pair is good.
   778	
   779	Suppose not, and choose a bad filling pair $(\sigma,M)$ with $|M|$
   780	minimal.  Let $v,e,f$ count the vertices, edges, and faces of $\sigma$.
   781	Then $e=3v-6$ and $f=2v-4$.
   782	The tetrahedral boundary is good, so $v>4$.
   783	Because taut fillings split under connected sum, we may assume that
   784	$\sigma$ is prime, meaning not a connected sum along a triangle.
   785	In particular, $\sigma$ has no vertex of degree $3$.
   786	
   787	Call a support tetrahedron $t$ of $M$ \emph{eligible} if two of its
   788	oriented boundary faces are faces of $X(\sigma)$ with the correct signs.
   789	It cannot have three such faces, since that would give a degree-$3$
   790	vertex in $\sigma$.
   791	By Proposition \ref{maxdeg},
   792	\[
   793	|M|=\Zvol(\sigma) \leq f-\maxdeg(\sigma).
   794	\]
   795	For each support tetrahedron $u$ of $M$, let $A(u)$ be the set of faces
   796	of $\sigma$ that occur as correctly oriented boundary faces of $u$.
   797	Each $A(u)$ has size at most $2$.
   798	Every face of $\sigma$ occurs in at least one $A(u)$, because its
   799	coefficient in $\partial M=X(\sigma)$ is nonzero with the prescribed sign.
   800	List the support tetrahedra of $M$ and reveal the sets $A(u)$ one at a
   801	time, recording only faces of $\sigma$ not previously seen.
   802	If $r$ support tetrahedra reveal two new faces, then
   803	\[
   804	f \leq |\operatorname{supp} M|+r \leq |M|+r.
   805	\]
   806	Thus $r\geq \maxdeg(\sigma)$.
   807	Consequently there are at least $\maxdeg(\sigma)$ eligible support
   808	tetrahedra whose pairs of boundary faces are mutually disjoint.
   809	We will use only the fact that, given any boundary face $s$ and any
   810	eligible tet $t$, there is another eligible tet whose boundary faces are
   811	disjoint from those of $t$ and do not include $s$.
   812	
   813	Let $t$ be an eligible tet with boundary faces
   814	$s_1=[a,b,c]$ and $s_2=[b,a,d]$.
   815	Removing $t$ from $M$ gives a taut filling $M-t$ of the cycle obtained
   816	from $X(\sigma)$ by replacing $s_1,s_2$ with
