"If sigma is a simplicial triangulation of S^2, any taut filling tau of sigma is a flag complex."
Flag (taut.tex:944-951): every clique in the 1-skeleton occurs as a simplex; for a
triangulation of S^2, flag <=> prime AND not the boundary of a tetrahedron.

PROOF (taut.tex:969-1037) rules out 3 taboo configs in tau:
  1. empty triangle: the 3 edges of {A,B,C} present, no 2-simplex ABC.
  2. empty K4: the 6 edges of {A,B,C,D} present, no 3-simplex ABCD.
  3. empty K5: the 10 edges of {A,B,C,D,E} present.
  Config 3 <= (1 & 2 ruled out): the K5 + its 5 associated 3-simplices = boundary of a
    4-simplex ~ S^3, "not possible as a subcomplex of the topological 3-ball tau".
    [THE topological step — must be replaced by a COMBINATORIAL obstruction.]
  Rule out 1,2: minimal counterexample + edge flip (remove an eligible tet) / deg-3 peel:
    deg-3 vertex => connected sum with a single tet, pull off => smaller counterexample;
    no deg-3 => maxdeg>=4 disjointly eligible tets, remove one = edge flip on sigma; case
    (a) result is still an S^2 triangulation (smaller ctrex directly), or case (b) becomes an
    almost-disjoint union of two triangulations joined along an edge (a K3/K4 then lands on
    one side => counterexample). Config-1: >=4 flip choices vs 3 forbidden edges. Config-2:
    octahedron handled as a special case => assume maxdeg>=5; at most 2 eligible tets contain
    a forbidden face ABC/BCD => >= maxdeg-2 good flips.
