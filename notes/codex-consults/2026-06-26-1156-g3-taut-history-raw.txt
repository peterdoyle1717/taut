   695	An \emph{$n$-simplex} $s$ is simply a set of size $n+1$.

   697	which are $k$-simplices with $-1 \leq k \leq n$,
   698	$-1$ being the dimension of the empty simplex.
   699	A \emph{simplicial complex} $\sigma$ is a finite collection of simplices
   700	closed under taking faces:
   701	If $t \subset s \in \sigma$ then $t \in \sigma$.
   702	
   703	For any $k$-simplex $s \in \sigma$ define the \emph{link}
   704	\[
   705	\link(s,\sigma) = \{t: t \cap s = \emptyset, s \cup t \in \sigma \}
   706	.
   707	\]
   708	This is a simplicial complex, but
   709	(except for $\link(\emptyset,\sigma)=\sigma$)
   710	it is not a subcomplex of $\sigma$,
   711	because we are taking simplices in $\sigma$ and knocking down
   712	their dimension by $k+1$.
   713	
   714	We are interested in particularly nice simplicial complexes
   715	called normal pseudomanifolds, or as we will prefer to say,
   716	\emph{clean $n$-complexes}.
   717	
   718	\begin{enumerate}
   719	\item
   720	To start with,
   721	a clean complex must be \emph{pure},
   722	meaning that every simplex of $\sigma$ belongs to some $n$-simplex in $\sigma$.
   723	To determine $\sigma$ we can specify its $n$-simplices,
   724	and then throw in all their subsets.
   725	So we can confound a pure complex with the set of its $n$-simplices.
   726	
   727	\item
   728	A clean complex is a \emph{pseudomanifold}:
   729	Every $n$-simplex abuts at most one other $n$-simplex
   730	across any given $n-1$-simplex.
   731	Another way to say this is that
   732	the link $\link(s,\sigma)$ of any $n-1$-simplex $s$
   733	consists of either a single point 
   734	(in which case $s \in \del \sigma$ is a \emph{boundary simplex}),
   735	or two points
   736	($s \in \sigma \setminus \del \sigma$ is an \emph{interior simplex}.)
   737	
   738	\item
   739	A clean complex is \emph{normal}:
   740	For any simplex $s$ of dimension $0,\ldots,n-2$,
