The proposed argument for (A) is correct, with one small formal precision: choose the second eligible tet by underlying 4-set, not by “copy.” Then `M - u` still has coefficient `2` on the 4-set `t`.

The manifold issue (B) is not precluded merely because `ab` is not an edge of `sigma_t`. That only says `s1={a,b,c}` and `s2={a,b,d}` are not boundary triangles of `M-t`. In a stickerball they could still be interior triangles a priori. So this needs a real argument.

A Lean-suitable formulation is:

Let `Good(N)` mean either one stickerball or two stickerballs joined along one edge. From the induction/minimality hypothesis:

For every eligible tet `u` of `M`, `M - u` is `Good`.

Also prove once:

`Good_no_double`: if `Good(N)`, every tet coefficient is at most `1`.

`Good_triangle_incidence`: if `Good(N)`, every triangle is incident to at most two distinct tets.

For the two-stickerball-along-edge case, the second lemma follows because a triangle cannot be split between the two pieces: their intersection is only an edge, so any common triangle would enlarge the intersection.

Now fix eligible `t={a,b,c,d}` with old boundary faces

```text
s1 = {a,b,c}
s2 = {a,b,d}
```

and gluing faces

```text
r1 = {a,c,d}
r2 = {b,c,d}.
```

By induction, `M - t` is `Good`. Therefore `r1,r2` are genuine boundary faces of `M-t`, and if the re-gluing is clean then adding `t` along `r1,r2` gives a stickerball by the shelling constructor. The only simpliciality failures to rule out are:

1. `t` already occurs in `M-t`.
2. `s1` or `s2` already occurs as an interior triangle of `M-t`.

For (A), suppose `t` already occurs in `M-t`. Then `M` has coefficient `2` on the tetrahedron `t`. Since `sigma` has no degree-3 vertex, `maxdeg(sigma) >= 4`, and there are at least `maxdeg(sigma)` pairwise disjoint eligible tets. Pick an eligible `u` whose underlying tetrahedron is not `t`. Then `M-u` still has coefficient `2` on `t`. But by minimality `M-u` is `Good`, contradicting `Good_no_double`.

For (B), suppose for example that `s1={a,b,c}` is already an interior triangle of `M-t`. Since `M-t` is `Good`, exactly two distinct tets of `M-t` contain `s1`; call them `p` and `q`. After re-adding `t`, the triangle `s1` is contained in the three distinct tets

```text
p, q, t.
```

This is not detected by coefficient multiplicity, because the three tets are distinct.

Choose an eligible `u` not among `{p,q,t}`. This is where the `>= maxdeg` supply is used: the forbidden set has size at most `3`, while `maxdeg(sigma) >= 4`. Then `M-u` still contains all three tets `p,q,t`, so `s1` is still incident to three distinct tetrahedra in `M-u`. By minimality, `M-u` is `Good`, contradicting `Good_triangle_incidence`.

The same argument applies if `s2` is over-incident. If both `s1` and `s2` are over-incident, choose one of them; you only need to preserve one three-tet triangle obstruction, so you do not need to avoid all tets incident to both faces.

So the exact answer to the crux is:

`ab ∉ sigma_t` does not force `s1,s2` to be new. It only forces them not to be boundary faces of `M-t`. Their possible interior presence is ruled out by the disjoint-eligible-tet persistence argument above.

One additional formal note: if your Lean definition of `stickerball` requires the last gluing intersection to be exactly the two faces `r1,r2`, then you must also rule out extra lower-dimensional intersection, especially an existing edge `ab` in `M-t`. That is a separate “clean intersection” lemma, not a triangle-incidence lemma. If your current theorem only formalizes the two stated simpliciality obligations, then (A) and (B) are the complete simpliciality argument.
