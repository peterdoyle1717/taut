TASK: Formulate correctly the simpliciality argument in Theorem 2 of the
Doyle-Ellison-Wang "taut fillings" paper (being formalized in Lean 4).  The
requester will then adversarially evaluate your proof and iterate with you.

DEFINITIONS.  A taut filling M of a triangulated 2-sphere sigma is an integral
3-chain (a +/-1 combination of tetrahedra = 4-vertex sets) with boundary the
fundamental cycle of sigma, of minimal L1 norm.  A STICKERBALL is a shellable
SIMPLICIAL complex (a genuine simplicial triangulation of the 3-ball): built from
one tetrahedron by gluing tets one at a time along 1 or 2 boundary faces.
"Simplicial" entails BOTH:
  (i) no tetrahedron occurs with multiplicity >= 2 (every coefficient in {-1,0,1});
  (ii) MANIFOLD: every triangle (2-face) lies in at most two tets of the support
       (no triangle is shared by three or more DISTINCT tets).
A stickerball may also be TWO stickerballs joined along a single shared edge.

THEOREM 2: every taut filling is a stickerball.  Proof by minimal counterexample:
(sigma,M) a bad pair (M taut, not a stickerball), |M| minimal; reduce to sigma
prime, so no degree-3 vertex.

ELIGIBLE TET / FLIP.  A tet t in M is eligible if it shares exactly two faces with
sigma.  Write t={a,b,c,d} with boundary faces s1={a,b,c}, s2={a,b,d} (sharing edge
ab); its other two faces are {a,c,d},{b,c,d}.  Removing t flips edge ab->cd: M-t
fills sigma_t = sigma with s1,s2 removed and {a,c,d},{b,c,d} added.  Cases:
  (1) cd not previously an edge: sigma_t is a 2-sphere; by minimality M-t is ONE
      stickerball.
  (2) cd already an edge: sigma_t is two 2-spheres joined along cd; by minimality
      M-t is TWO stickerballs joined along edge cd.
There are at least maxdeg(sigma) disjoint eligible tets (disjoint boundary-face pairs).

Re-glue t onto M-t by attaching along its two faces {a,c,d},{b,c,d} (which lie on
sigma_t), so t's two faces s1={a,b,c}, s2={a,b,d} become the new boundary.
M = (M-t) + t.

THE QUESTION.  Formulate rigorously WHY M is a stickerball (the contradiction).
Enumerate ALL ways re-gluing t could fail to yield a stickerball, and rule out each
using minimality + the (>= maxdeg) disjoint eligible tets.  Address precisely:

  (A) Doubled-tet failure: t already occurs in M-t, so 2t in M.  Proposed: pick a
      disjoint eligible u (u != t); M-u still contains 2t; M-u is one stickerball or
      two joined along an edge, and 2t lies wholly in one stickerball piece -- but a
      stickerball has all multiplicities <= 1 -- contradiction.  Confirm or correct.

  (B) Manifold failure: could ONE OR BOTH of t's two new boundary faces s1={a,b,c},
      s2={a,b,d} ALREADY be present in M-t as an interior triangle (each sitting in
      two tets of M-t)?  If so, M would have that triangle in THREE distinct tets
      (no tet doubled, so (A)'s multiplicity argument does NOT detect it).  Is (B)
      genuinely possible, or precluded?
        - If precluded, give the EXACT reason (edge ab is not in sigma_t; does that,
          plus M-t being a stickerball, force s1,s2 to be new?).  Do not merely
          assert "the glue is clean".
        - If possible, rule it out.  Does it need a disjoint eligible u avoiding ALL
          tets incident to the over-incident triangle (hence the >= maxdeg count, not
          just a pair), or does the same "M-u is a stickerball" contradiction work
          with only a disjoint pair?

Give a rigorous formulation suitable for Lean formalization, distinguishing what
follows from the induction hypothesis (M minus any eligible tet is a stickerball or
two joined along an edge) versus what needs a fresh argument.  Be concrete; do not
hand-wave the manifold/cleanliness point -- that is the crux.
